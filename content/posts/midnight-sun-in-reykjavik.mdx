Iceland in late winter is a study in restraint. The light arrives late, leaves early, and everything in between feels borrowed.

I landed in Reykjavík with a soft bag, a 35mm prime, and no hard plan beyond walking until the cold made sense. The harbor wind cut sideways. Color lived in the roofs — corrugated reds and greens against black volcanic stone.

## Harbor mornings

Before the cafés opened, I circled the old harbor. Fishermen moved like they had always known this weather. Tourists appeared later, layered in rental parkas, pointing at the Harpa's glass lattice as if it might explain the island.

> The cold doesn't ask permission. It just arrives, then waits to see who stays.

## Roads west

A day trip toward Snæfellsnes taught me how quickly Iceland changes character. One ridge of moss, then lava fields like broken pottery, then a church alone on a hill with the Atlantic as its congregation.

## What I keep

I keep the sound of wind through a rented car window left cracked on purpose. I keep the taste of lamb stew after a day of gray. And I keep the reminder that some places don't perform for you — they simply continue, whether or not you write them down.
