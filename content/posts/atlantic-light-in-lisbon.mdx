Lisbon's light is famous for a reason. It arrives sideways in the afternoon, painting tiles and laundry lines with the same generosity.

I spent a week climbing and descending — Alfama's stairs, Belém's waterfront, a miradouro at golden hour where strangers shared wine from plastic cups.

## Tram 28, once

Ride it once for the postcard. Then walk. The city is better when your legs set the pace and you can stop for a pastel without calculating the next stop.

## What the river does

The Tagus widens the sky. At sunset the water becomes a second city — inverted roofs, trembling lights, ferries drawing soft lines across the frame.

> Cities by the water teach patience. Tide and tram schedules both arrive when they arrive.

This was a short trip with a long aftertaste — the kind that sends you looking at flights again before you've fully unpacked.
