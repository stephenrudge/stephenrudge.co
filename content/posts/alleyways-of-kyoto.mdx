Kyoto rewards the people who leave the main arteries. The famous gates are worth seeing — but the city reveals itself in lanes barely wide enough for two umbrellas.

## Morning near Philosopher's Path

I walked early, before tour buses. Stone lanterns still wet from overnight rain. A shopkeeper arranged persimmons with the care of someone who has done this for decades and still cares how they look.

## How to wander (without a checklist)

1. Pick a neighborhood, not a monument.
2. Follow any street that smells like wood smoke or incense.
3. When you feel lost, sit for tea. The map will still be there later.

> Getting lost in Kyoto isn't failure. It's the itinerary writing itself.

## Field note

Bring soft shoes. Kyoto is a walking city, and the best photographs happen at elbow height — doorways, noren curtains, steam from a ramen shop at dusk.
