Utah's desert doesn't whisper. It waits until you're alone on a two-lane road, then opens the horizon like a door you didn't know was closed.

I drove into Moab with dust on the windshield and a cooler of half-melted ice. The plan was simple: wake early, chase light, sleep when the stars showed up.

## Arches before the crowds

At dawn, Delicate Arch sits like a punctuation mark against cold sky. The trail is familiar if you've seen the photos — but standing under it, the scale resets your sense of what "rock" means.

> Red stone holds heat the way memory holds a place. Even after sunset, the walls keep a little of the day.

## The highway as ritual

Between parks, the highway became the trip. Gas stations with handwritten coffee prices. Radio stations fading into static. The kind of silence that makes you notice your own thoughts.

## Leaving

I left Moab slower than I arrived. Road trips end the way good sentences do — not with a flourish, but with a period that feels earned.
